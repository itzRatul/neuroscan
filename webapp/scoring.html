NeuroScan collapses every measurement into one number on a 0–100 scale. The weights and thresholds are not arbitrary — they trace back to the relative clinical sensitivity of each stroke sign.

The math

The scoring formula.

Three asymmetry components (smile, eye close, eye open) are combined with weights that reflect their clinical importance, then scaled to 0–100. 

movement_score =
    smile_asymmetry  × 0.50   # Smile is the strongest single FAST predictor
  + eye_open_asym    × 0.25   # Eyebrow / eyelid weakness
  + eye_close_asym   × 0.25   # Lagophthalmos sign

final_score = movement_score × 100   # 0–100 scale
Score ≤ 30
NORMAL

Facial movement appears symmetric. No stroke-related asymmetry detected in this screening. Continue routine health monitoring.

Score 31 – 60
MODERATE

Mild asymmetry detected. Re-run the test. If other FAST symptoms appear (arm weakness, slurred speech), consult a clinician without delay.

Score > 60
HIGH RISK

Significant asymmetry detected — a possible sign of stroke. Call your local emergency number immediately. Time is brain.

Why 50 / 25 / 25?

The mouth-droop sign carries roughly twice the diagnostic weight of either eye sign in published validation of the Cincinnati Prehospital Stroke Scale (Kothari et al., 1999). Mouth-droop alone identifies ~72% of strokes; eye-related findings add the remaining sensitivity.

Our 50 / 25 / 25 split mirrors that relative sensitivity. We considered a 60 / 20 / 20 split closer to the raw numbers — but reduced the smile dominance slightly so that bilateral findings (smile and eyes) can still push the score into the moderate band, which is the safer behaviour for an early-warning tool.

The 30 / 60 threshold breakpoints come from internal calibration on the labelled facial-droop datasets described on the next page.
