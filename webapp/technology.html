The model that powers
every NeuroScan test.

NeuroScan stands on Google's MediaPipe Face Landmarker — a research-grade computer-vision model deployed in Google Meet, YouTube, and Android. Here is exactly how it works and why we picked it.

The core model

Google MediaPipe Face Landmarker.

468 landmarks · 30 FPS

A two-stage neural network.

MediaPipe Face Landmarker runs in two passes. First, a lightweight detector (BlazeFace) finds the face region. Second, a mesh regressor predicts the 3D position of 468 anatomical landmarks across the face.

The whole pipeline runs in 8–15 ms per frame on consumer hardware thanks to WebAssembly + WebGL acceleration. The model file is only 3.7 MB and is fetched once, then cached by the browser.

Trained on roughly 30,000 diverse facial images with 3D mesh ground truth, the model handles a wide range of skin tones, ages, and lighting conditions out of the box.

Why we trust it

Three reasons we built on MediaPipe.

Production-grade

What we actually use

Of 468 landmarks, we read just nine.

MediaPipe gives us a full face mesh. The scoring math only needs the points that correspond to muscles innervated by the facial nerve (cranial nerve VII).

Everything in NeuroScan

The full technology stack.

Static, client-side, by design.

Every component runs in your browser. No backend, no API key, no database. Open the browser's network tab during a test — you will see model assets fetched once, then silence. No frames, no landmarks, no scores leave your machine.

That makes privacy provable instead of promised. You can verify the whole stack with a single right-click.
