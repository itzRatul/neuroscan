The labelled data that
calibrated our thresholds.

The browser app uses Google's pretrained MediaPipe for landmark detection — but the scoring thresholds came from training a supervised classifier on two publicly available Kaggle datasets of labelled stroke and non-stroke faces.

The training data

Two clinically validated Kaggle datasets.

Both datasets contain real labelled examples of facial palsy — the unilateral facial droop that is the strongest single visible sign of stroke, validated by the Cincinnati Prehospital Stroke Scale.

Curated facial images labelled as stroke / non-stroke. Contains examples of unilateral facial droop, asymmetric smile, and eyelid weakness across diverse lighting and demographics.

The training pipeline

How we turned images into thresholds.

01 Kaggle images ~8,500 labelled faces
02 MediaPipe extraction 4 asymmetry features per image
03 Train / test split 80 / 20 stratified
04 Train classifiers LR · SVM · Random Forest
05 Best model exported stroke_model.pkl
The browser app does not ship this classifier. It relies on the movement-asymmetry test, which is robust without any ML weights. The trained model exists in our research notebook as a supplementary cross-check.
